Imagine building one of the world's most loved toy brands… then almost losing everything.

By 2004, LEGO was in its worst financial crisis. Its business had become complicated, expensive, and increasingly difficult to manage. That year, it reported a net loss of nearly two billion Danish kroner.

LEGO had expanded into too many directions. More products, more complexity, more costs. It was trying to become everything, while losing focus on what made it special.

Then came a major change. In 2004, Jørgen Vig Knudstorp became CEO. His mission wasn't to make LEGO bigger in every direction. It was to make the business stronger.

LEGO started cutting unnecessary complexity, selling non-core assets, reducing costs, and returning its attention to its core product: the brick. It also worked to reconnect with the customers who loved it.

The turnaround worked. By 2015, LEGO's revenue had reached 35.8 billion Danish kroner. A company that had once faced a serious fight for survival had rebuilt itself into one of the world's leading toy businesses.

Here's the lesson: growth isn't always about doing more. Sometimes, it's about getting back to what you do best.
